Give an agent a credential and it can spend. Nothing between it and the rail asks whether it
should. The card sees a valid card, the API key sees a valid key, and both are right, because
neither was ever told what this agent is for.

<Info>
  **The decision boundary.** The moment something non-human can initiate spend you would want
  to review, you are at the layer this product serves. Before that, a hard-coded cap in your
  own code is fine. After it, the question is not whether you have a control layer but
  whether it can be bypassed, and whether it can prove what it permitted.
</Info>

## Two products, one host

**Spend control** is the thing that is told. Before the agent pays a vendor or calls a paid
tool, it asks, and gets an answer in the same request: allowed, needs a person, or denied,
naming the rule that decided. Allowed answers come back signed, so whatever executes can
verify the authority instead of trusting the agent that carries it. Kordio holds no funds, no
accounts, and none of your credentials.

**The ledger** is a programmable double-entry system of record. Accounts, balanced postings,
derived balances, point-in-time history, and reconciliation as a first-class operation. It
stands entirely on its own, and plenty of teams want it for reasons that have nothing to do
with agents.

The control layer decides what may happen; the ledger records what did. Each is a complete
API on its own. The control layer just happens to be the ledger's first customer, which is
the reason to trust both: the ledger is general enough that a real product was built on it
without special-casing, and the control layer's books are the real thing rather than an audit
log wearing accounting vocabulary.

## Is this for you?

**Yes, if something non-human can start a payment or a paid API call.** An agent buying
supplies, booking freight, managing ad budget, or just burning through model and tool spend
at a rate nobody predicted. You want a rule that runs before the money moves and a record
afterwards that names what permitted it. That is [spend control](/agents/why).

**Yes, separately, if you need an auditable money system of record.** A treasury product, a
marketplace splitting funds, an FX desk, a SaaS company billing its own usage. That is the
[ledger](/ledger/why), which comes included on the higher plans and is available as an add-on on
the rest.

**No, if you only need bookkeeping for your own company's taxes and statements.** That is
what accounting software is for. Reach for QuickBooks or Xero.
